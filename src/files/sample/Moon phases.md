# Moon phases

The Sun always lights half of the Moon. Its phase is how much of that lit half we can see from Earth, and the cycle repeats about every 29.5 days.

| Phase | What you see | Rises, and sets (roughly) |
|---|---|---|
| New Moon | Nothing: the lit side faces away from us | With the Sun |
| Waxing crescent | A thin sliver, growing | In the morning; sets after dark |
| First quarter | Half of the face | Around midday; sets around midnight |
| Waxing gibbous | More than half, growing | In the afternoon; sets before sunrise |
| Full Moon | The whole face | At sunset; sets at sunrise |
| Waning gibbous | More than half, shrinking | After sunset; sets after sunrise |
| Third quarter | Half of the face | Around midnight; sets around midday |
| Waning crescent | A thin sliver, shrinking | Before sunrise; sets in the afternoon |

Waxing means growing, and waning means shrinking. For exact times, check local predictions.

## For observing

Moonlight washes out faint sights, so the darkest skies come while the Moon is down: around new Moon, or after moonset. That's why the [[Observing plan]] starts after moonset. A first quarter Moon is high in the evening sky and a fine sight of its own.

Source: NASA Science, "Moon Phases".
