# Orion

Best from the dark site; see the [[Observing plan]].

Orion, the Hunter, is one of the easiest constellations to find: look for three stars in a short, straight line, Orion's Belt.

## Stars

- **Betelgeuse**, at Orion's shoulder, is a red supergiant.
- **Rigel**, at his foot, is a blue supergiant, hotter than Betelgeuse, and the brightest star in Orion. The two colours show even to the eye.
- **The Belt** is Alnitak, Alnilam and Mintaka. They look alike from here, but they lie at very different distances: about 800, 1,300 and 900 light-years.

## The Orion Nebula

Below the Belt hangs Orion's Sword, and in it the Orion Nebula, M42: a cloud of dust and gas where new stars are forming, about 1,500 light-years away. From a dark site you can see it without binoculars. At its heart, four massive young stars called the Trapezium shape the nebula.

## Finding your way

From the Northern Hemisphere, follow the Belt to the left and slightly down to reach Sirius, the brightest star in the night sky. Follow it the other way to orange Aldebaran in Taurus, and on towards the Pleiades.

Rigel is one corner of the [[Winter hexagon.excalidraw|winter hexagon]], and Betelgeuse sits inside it.
